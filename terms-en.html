4. How wishes and the shared world work

You select a wish from a prepared catalogue. The first test version offers five groups and 27 wishes. The system routes it randomly to an eligible participant, taking account of delivery time limits, availability, quiet mode and daily limits. A selected city is required for sending and ordinary receipt. In the current catalogue, all wishes are suitable for any time of day; time-of-day matching is not yet applied.

The recipient is not shown the sender's name. You cannot select a specific person, reply to the sender or hold a free-form conversation. The next send enters the allocation process again. This does not mean that the operator's technical records have no service-level links between them; see the policy for details.

A wish may wait for an eligible recipient. Under the test arrangement, it may be delivered to the MYVO operator as a special recipient if no ordinary recipient is found in time. This role is not a function that an ordinary participant can enable for themselves. A particular recipient, exact delivery time, receipt of every Android notification or a reply from another person cannot be promised.

Collective light and shared lights reflect activity under the test rules. They are not a monetary reward, a competition with an obligation to achieve a result, or a promise of a specific change in how you feel. A user's city represents the selected city association, not a verified precise location. The phone's time zone is taken into account separately.

Current limits and available functions are shown in the app. During the test, we may fix bugs and change parameters and function availability, communicating material changes in an appropriate way. This does not authorise the unannounced introduction of paid services or new processing purposes.
